For Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024 home cockpits

Your sim's cockpit displays, on real screens.

CockpitCast puts the glass displays of MSFS 2024 (PFD, MFD, radios, CDU and more) on extra monitors: on the sim PC itself, or on PCs, Raspberry Pis and tablets on your network. It draws classic steam gauges from live sim data. No pop-outs. No window capture.

Download for Windows View on GitHub

Free and open source (GPL-3.0). Windows sim PC; displays on Windows, Linux, Raspberry Pi and macOS. Android and iOS apps are coming later.

Built for home cockpits

Set it up once. After that, your display screens follow whatever aircraft you load.

🖥️

No pop-out windows

Displays come straight out of the sim's own DirectX 12 rendering. Nothing to pop out, move or resize, and no monitor on the sim PC given up for it.

🔀

Glass or steam, by itself

Load a glass cockpit and your screens show its PFD and MFD. Load a steam-gauge aircraft and they switch to gauge layouts within seconds.

🧠

Learns every aircraft

Every display the sim draws becomes available the first time you fly an aircraft: G1000, G3000, airliner screens, radio and autopilot readouts, even the hour meter.

🧩

Mix glass and steam

Design layouts in the browser: put a cropped piece of any display, like just the ADF frequency, right next to steam gauges.

⚡

One PC is enough

Monitors on the sim PC show screens with only the host installed: no extra app, no network, straight from the graphics card. Two screens can share one monitor, side by side.

📡

Or any screen on your network

Old mini PCs and Raspberry Pis. The display app finds the sim PC by itself, starts full screen and needs no keyboard.

🪶

Light on the sim

Only what your screens show is captured. Nothing watched means nothing captured, and the sim's memory is left alone.

🔄

Updates itself

One click on the sim PC installs a new version there and on every Windows display PC. No new installers to download.

✈️

Per-aircraft screens

Give any aircraft its own layout on any screen, like a TBM panel on screen 2. The rest keep switching by themselves.

Glass and steam in one layout

Crop any part of any display and place it among steam gauges. Here the G1000's engine strip sits next to a classic six-pack, all live.

Steam gauges are drawn by CockpitCast from SimConnect data: airspeed, attitude, altimeter, turn coordinator, heading, VSI, VOR/ILS, ADF, clock and engine dials.

A layout editor that remembers every aircraft

The editor is part of the CockpitCast window on the sim PC. Its Insert window shows the displays of the aircraft you are flying, live, and every display you have flown before, grouped by aircraft.

Pick a display, drag a box around the part you want, and save. Every screen using that layout updates at once.

How it works

Installed in a few minutes. Step-by-step: the guides.

Install the host on the sim PC

It sits in the tray, builds a small Community package for MSFS from your own install, and captures the displays your screens need.

Choose where each screen goes

A monitor on the sim PC: pick it under On this PC in the Screens section. Nothing else to install. Another device: install the display app there and pick "Screen 1", "Screen 2"… once.

Fly

Screens show glass displays or steam gauges depending on the aircraft. Fine-tune anything in the CockpitCast window.

Everything in one window

Status, screens, layouts, the sim add-on, help and settings in one window, opened from the tray icon. A status bar shows at a glance whether the sim, the add-on and the capture are working.

Each screen goes to a monitor on the sim PC or to a display device, and any aircraft can have its own choices. How to set up screens.
