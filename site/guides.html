Guides

From install to a cockpit that switches by itself between aircraft.

What to install

It depends on where your cockpit screens are.

Your screens are on...Install
Monitors connected to the sim PCOnly the host on the sim PC. Nothing else: screens are drawn straight from the graphics card. See Screens on the sim PC.
Another PC, a Raspberry Pi or a tabletThe host on the sim PC, and the display app on each of those devices. See Display devices.
BothBoth. Each screen goes either to a monitor on the sim PC or to a device.

Install and first flight

  1. Close MSFS. Setup installs a small add-on (the hook) into the sim's Community folder, and the sim reads add-ons only when it starts.
  2. Run CockpitCast-Host-Setup.exe on the sim PC. No admin rights needed: start it normally, not "as administrator".
  3. On the MSFS Community folder page, check the folder. Setup fills in the one it found. With MSFS Addons Linker, choose the Community folder from its options (where it puts the links), not your add-ons library.
  4. Finish with Start CockpitCast now ticked. The CockpitCast window opens, and an icon appears in the tray (under the ^ arrow at first: drag it onto the taskbar to keep it visible).
  5. Start MSFS and load a flight. About 10 seconds after the flight is live, CockpitCast starts capturing.
Antivirus warning? CockpitCast is not code-signed yet, and its capture module loads into the sim, which antivirus programs watch closely. A check like AVG's CyberCapture is not a finding: let it finish.

The CockpitCast window

Open it from the tray icon (click it, or right-click > Open CockpitCast), or start CockpitCast from the Start menu. The status bar at the bottom always shows the version, the sim, the add-on, the capture and the display apps.

  • Status: what is going on, and anything that needs fixing.
  • Screens: what each screen shows, where, and per aircraft.
  • Layouts: design steam gauge layouts.
  • Sim add-on: the hook in the Community folder; reinstall it after sim updates.
  • Help: fixes for common problems, logs, the connected display apps.
  • Settings: updates, beta versions, start with Windows.

Screens

A screen is one place in your cockpit that shows something: a monitor, half a monitor, a tablet.

  1. Open Screens. You start with Screen 1 and Screen 2. Add screen adds more; name them as you like ("Left", "Engine panel").
  2. In All aircraft, pick for each screen:
    • Glass: the display it shows in glass cockpits. Automatic takes the cockpit's own displays in order, PFD first, so G1000, G3000 and airliner screens work without setup.
    • Layout: the steam gauge layout it shows in aircraft with round dials.
  3. Choose where the screen appears: a monitor of the sim PC (On this PC), or a display device.
  4. Press Save.

From then on, screens switch by themselves: load a glass aircraft and they show its displays; load a steam aircraft and they show your layouts within seconds.

Glass displays in this cockpit, under the table, lists what the loaded aircraft draws. Mark a display as primary to make Automatic use it, or add a smaller instrument (a radio, the hour meter) as a display.

Screens on the sim PC

For monitors connected to the sim PC. Only the host is needed: no display app, no network.

  1. In Screens, set the screen's On this PC to a monitor.
  2. Pick the Area: the whole monitor, or a half (left, right, top, bottom). Put the PFD on the left half and the MFD on the right half to share one small monitor.
  3. Press Save. The screen appears straight away, as a borderless window.

Glass displays are drawn straight from the graphics card: no compression, no delay. Steam gauge layouts are drawn with live sim data.

  • Local screens: on/off (Screens, or Show local screens in the tray menu) turns them all on or off, keeping their monitors.
  • Local screens: always on top keeps them in front of everything, also the taskbar. normal windows makes them behave like other windows, in Alt+Tab.
  • Right-click a local screen for Turn local screens off or Open CockpitCast. Clicking a screen never takes the keyboard from the sim.
Tip: in Windows Settings > Personalization > Taskbar > Taskbar behaviors, turn off Show my taskbar on all displays, so the taskbar stays off your cockpit monitors. The monitors must be on the same graphics card as the sim.
Careful with the main monitor: a screen set to your main monitor covers it. Right-click the screen to turn local screens off.

Display devices

A mini PC, a Raspberry Pi or a tablet next to your cockpit monitors, on the same network as the sim PC.

  1. Install the display app: CockpitCast-Client-Setup.exe on Windows, or the Linux and Raspberry Pi downloads.
  2. Start it. On its Settings page:
    • Host: leave it on Find automatically. It finds the sim PC by itself (or type the sim PC's address).
    • Windows on this device: one window per monitor; pick the monitor and which screen it shows (Screen 1, Screen 2...), and full screen.
    • Startup: Start when I sign in and Open the windows straight away, for a box without a keyboard.
  3. Press Save and start. Tap or click a display to get back to Settings.

The first time, allow CockpitCast through the Windows firewall on private networks on the sim PC. Help in the CockpitCast window lists the connected display apps; Identify shows an app's name on its screen.

Steam gauge layouts

What screens show in aircraft with round dials: a six-pack, an engine panel, or a mix with parts of glass displays.

  1. Open Layouts and press New (or Duplicate an existing one).
  2. Set its Size to your screen's shape: 1280 × 720 for 16:9, 1024 × 768 for 4:3. The layout is scaled to fit each screen.
  3. Press Insert gauge or display… and pick a gauge: airspeed, attitude, altimeter, turn coordinator, heading, vertical speed, VOR/ILS, ADF, clock, or a dial for engine and fuel values (presets for RPM, oil, fuel and more).
  4. Drag gauges to move them; drag the corner to resize. Click one to edit it on the right: for a dial, its value, range, Numbers every, Ticks every and colour arcs.
  5. Press Save (or Ctrl+S). Every screen using the layout updates at once.
  6. In Screens, pick the layout for a screen.

A piece of a glass display

Insert a glass display, then drag a box on its picture in the settings on the right to keep only part of it, like the ADF frequency or the G1000 engine strip, next to your steam gauges.

Demo animates the gauges with a built-in flight, so you can design without the sim running; Live data uses the sim.

Per-aircraft screens

Give one aircraft its own choices, like a TBM 930 panel on screen 2, while the others keep the normal rules.

  1. In Screens, the aircraft you are flying always has a column (marked loaded now). For another aircraft you have flown, choose it in Set up another aircraft….
  2. In that aircraft's column, pick a layout or glass display for any screen. Cells left on Same as all aircraft follow the normal rules.
  3. Press Save. Whenever that aircraft is loaded, its screens show your choice straight away.

Reset clears the loaded aircraft's choices; Remove removes another aircraft's column. Status shows what each screen shows in the loaded aircraft, marked chosen for this aircraft.

Sim updates and the add-on

The hook holds a copy of one sim file, so after every MSFS update, reinstall it: close MSFS, open Sim add-on, press Reinstall hook. CockpitCast warns in the status bar when the hook is outdated or not detected.

  • Community folder elsewhere (another drive, MS Store version, Addons Linker): enter it in Sim add-on and press Save. CockpitCast then always uses that folder and never switches to another one by itself.
  • Reinstall does not work? At the bottom of Sim add-on: a ready-made command, or Build the hook and copy it into the Community folder yourself.

CockpitCast updates

CockpitCast checks for new versions by itself. When one is out, the status bar says so; Settings > Install and restart updates the sim PC and every Windows display PC with the installed display app. Beta versions in Settings also offers test versions before a release.

Problems

Help in the CockpitCast window has fixes for the common ones: glass displays not appearing, which Community folder MSFS uses, antivirus warnings, a screen covering what you need.

For anything else, open an issue on GitHub and attach the logs: Help > Logs has a command that zips them, with Windows' crash records, onto your desktop.
